# Paper Structure and Argument Check

Check only the full text, section, or paragraph the user specifies. Diagnose first; rewrite only when requested. Do not start a complete research workflow.

## Approach

1. Identify the central question and main answer from the available material. If information is insufficient, state a provisional understanding and identify the gaps.
2. Use a reverse outline only when cross-paragraph order, repetition, or paragraph function is an issue: summarize each relevant paragraph's function and claim in one sentence, locating repetition, excessive background, missing transitions, and irrelevant paragraphs. For a single paragraph, first identify the exact pair of sentences and the missing inference between them. If no issue affects the argument, one sentence saying so is enough. Retain necessary methodological detail rather than deleting it uniformly for brevity.
3. Check whether the research gap explains what is known, what remains unresolved, why it matters, and how this paper responds. Missing citations in the current review do not establish that nobody in the field has studied the topic.
4. Distinguish novelty, theoretical contribution, methodological contribution, empirical contribution, and practical significance. Retain only the types supported by the material; do not require every paper to make every type of contribution.
5. When useful, map research question → data/variables → method → results → conclusion. Identify unanswered questions, results without a methodological basis, and conclusions beyond the study's scope.
6. If a literature-review diagnosis is requested, check whether it is organized around questions, findings, and disagreements rather than paper-by-paper summaries. Do not invent reasons for disagreements.

## Output

Lead with the most consequential issues. Give the location, basis, and smallest useful revision for each. List issues directly for short texts; add a paragraph outline or alignment table for longer sections when useful. Do not force changes to sound paragraphs.

## Example requests

“Check the logic of this introduction without rewriting it yet.”
“Align the research questions, methods, and conclusions, and identify unanswered questions.”
